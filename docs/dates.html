Dates & Times

Snippets can type today’s date, the time, or a date in the future or past, in any format.

Ready-made examples

Examples are for Sunday, 1 November 2026 at 9:05 AM.

Snippet contentTypes
%B %e, %YNovember 1, 2026 (the starter snippet ddate)
%1I:%M %p9:05 AM (the starter snippet ttime)
%e %B %Y1 November 2026
%d/%m/%Y01/11/2026
%m/%d/%y11/01/26
%Y-%m-%d2026-11-01
%A, %B %eSunday, November 1
%a %e %bSun 1 Nov
%H:%M09:05
%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S2026-11-01 09:05:00
Day %j of %YDay 305 of 2026

The full list of codes is in the Macro Reference. In the snippet editor, use Insert ▸ Date and Insert ▸ Time to pick them from a menu.

Month and weekday names and AM/PM follow your Mac’s language and region (System Settings ▸ General ▸ Language & Region).

Any format you like

%date:pattern% uses standard Unicode date patterns (Insert ▸ Date ▸ Custom format (Unicode)…).

Snippet contentTypes
%date:yyyy-MM-dd%2026-11-01
%date:EEEE d MMMM%Sunday 1 November
%date:'Week' w%Week 45 in the US (week 44 where weeks start on Monday)
%date:QQQ yyyy%Q4 2026
%date:h:mm a%9:05 AM

In patterns, MM is the month and mm the minute. The pattern can’t contain a %.

Dates in the future or past

Put %@, a + or -, a number and a unit before the date codes. Every date code after it is shifted.

UnitLetterExample
YearsY%@+1Y
MonthsM (capital)%@+3M
DaysD%@+15D
Hoursh%@+2h
Minutesm (small)%@+30m
Secondss%@+45s

Examples

Snippet contentTypes
Payment due %@+30D%B %e, %YPayment due December 1, 2026
Yesterday (%@-1D%A)Yesterday (Saturday)
Back at %@+2h%1I:%M %pBack at 11:05 AM
Offer ends %@+1M%e %BOffer ends 1 December

Need today and a later date in one snippet? Put today’s codes first, then %@ and the later date: Sent %d/%m. Reply by %@+7D%d/%m. types “Sent 01/11. Reply by 08/11.”

Only one shift is active at a time: a second %@ replaces the first (it doesn’t add to it), and it is counted from the current time.
